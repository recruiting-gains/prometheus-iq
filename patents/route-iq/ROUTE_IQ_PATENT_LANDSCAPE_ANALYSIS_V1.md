## 2. Route IQ Technical Baseline

Route IQ is documented as a computer-implemented adaptive representative intelligence system.

Core architecture:

Representative -> Evidence Collection -> Learning Engine -> Representative Profile -> Territory Intelligence -> Customer Intelligence -> Outcome Intelligence -> Probability Engine -> Recommendation Engine -> Representative

Key disclosed technical features:

- Representative-specific learning rather than treating field representatives as interchangeable.
- Evidence collection from representative activity, visits, customer interactions, and outcomes.
- Separate intelligence layers for representative, territory, customer, and outcomes.
- Continuous feedback from accepted, rejected, completed, modified, or outcome-producing recommendations.
- Probability scoring and recommendation generation based on updated intelligence state.
- Adaptive recommendations for a specific representative in a specific territory/customer/outcome context.
- Routing, CRM, maps, enrichment, and navigation as possible inputs or interfaces rather than the core invention.

## 10. Areas Where Route IQ Appears Technically Differentiated

Based on the public materials reviewed, Route IQ appears technically differentiated in the following non-legal, engineering sense:

1. Representative as a learned entity.

Route IQ models a representative's observed strengths, weaknesses, evidence quality, response behavior, territory fit, customer-category fit, and outcome patterns. Many located systems plan routes, assign leads, or recommend actions, but they appear less focused on continuously learning the individual representative as a dynamic intelligence object.

2. Four-layer intelligence architecture.

Route IQ separates representative intelligence, territory intelligence, customer intelligence, and outcome intelligence, then feeds those layers into probability and recommendation logic. Located route-planning systems often focus on geography, stops, schedules, and route cost. Located sales recommendation systems often focus on account targeting, messaging, or next-best-action decisioning without the same field-representative loop.

3. Evidence-driven feedback loop.

Route IQ captures representative actions, accepted/rejected recommendations, customer interactions, outcomes, and evidence quality as inputs for future recommendations. Several systems include feedback or analytics, but the reviewed materials do not appear to combine feedback into the same representative-territory-customer-outcome learning cycle.

4. Route as interface, not core invention.

Route IQ is not framed as route optimization. Route sequencing, maps, CRM, enrichment, and navigation can be inputs or surfaces. The invention baseline is the adaptive intelligence architecture that decides what should be recommended to a specific representative after learning from evidence and outcomes.

5. Outcome-aware probability recommendations.

Route IQ's probability engine evaluates candidate actions from updated intelligence layers and returns adaptive recommendations. Many systems optimize routes, rank leads, assign tasks, or recommend next actions, but Route IQ ties probability recommendations to field outcomes and representative-specific learning.
