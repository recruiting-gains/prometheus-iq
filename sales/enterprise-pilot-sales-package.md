# Enterprise Pilot Sales Package

Purpose: give the Founder a complete buyer-facing and founder-facing package for selling Prometheus Route IQ pilots to enterprise and multi-location outside-sales organizations.

Prometheus Route IQ should be positioned as the AI operating system for outside sales: a governed way to decide where field reps should go, verify intentional field work, capture outcomes, learn from the field, and expand what works across teams and locations.

This package supports:

- Fitness
- Roofing
- Security
- Medical
- Insurance
- General Outside Sales

## Package Boundary

Sales may use this package to explain value, qualify buyers, run discovery, structure pilots, discuss ROI, handle objections, guide pricing, define success criteria, and recommend post-pilot expansion.

Sales must not modify or imply ownership over Companion, Website, CRM runtime, Mobile, Dashboard, Infrastructure, production learning, production scoring, or production deployment.

## Buyer-Facing Package

### Enterprise Pilot Positioning

Buyer-facing statement:

Prometheus Route IQ helps outside-sales organizations run a controlled pilot that proves whether field teams can spend time in better places, capture cleaner field evidence, improve follow-up, and give managers clearer visibility into what actually produces.

Enterprise framing:

- This is a controlled pilot, not an uncontrolled rollout.
- The pilot tests operating value before a larger deployment.
- Prometheus is not replacing CRM.
- Prometheus is not a surveillance product.
- Prometheus uses Operating Profiles so the same outside-sales engine can speak the customer's industry language.
- Pilot success is judged by adoption, field activity quality, manager visibility, measurable outcomes, and expansion readiness.

Buyer talk track:

`The first goal is not to force a company-wide change. The first goal is to run a controlled pilot with approved users, clear data boundaries, and success criteria that show whether Prometheus Route IQ improves field execution for your team.`

### Operating Profile Explanation

Buyer-facing explanation:

An Operating Profile is the industry configuration that translates Prometheus Route IQ into the customer's field-sales language. It defines the words, outcomes, success events, and reporting labels for the pilot while preserving the same core outside-sales operating system.

Examples:

| Operating Profile | Success language | Common field outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitness | Membership opportunity | tour, trial pass, membership, referral |
| Roofing | Roofing opportunity | inspection, estimate, contract |
| Security | Security opportunity | site survey, quote, contract |
| Medical | Provider opportunity | appointment, referral, agreement |
| Insurance | Insurance opportunity | appointment, policy, referral |
| General Outside Sales | Qualified opportunity | conversation, appointment, contract, sale |

Buyer-safe boundary:

Operating Profiles adapt vocabulary, reporting, and pilot success language. They do not expose proprietary recommendation logic, change production scoring by themselves, or require a customer to replace existing CRM systems.

### Pilot Qualification Criteria

A pilot is qualified when all criteria are true:

1. The customer has recurring outside-sales activity.
2. The team has a measurable field outcome.
3. A manager or operator can drive adoption.
4. The pilot has an executive sponsor or clear approval path.
5. Data boundaries can be defined before launch.
6. Success criteria can be reviewed weekly.
7. A successful pilot has a realistic paid rollout path.

Disqualifiers:

- No outside-sales motion.
- No measurable field outcome.
- No manager accountable for adoption.
- No willingness to define pilot scope.
- The buyer only wants CRM replacement.
- The buyer requires unsupported integrations before any pilot value can be proven.
- Privacy, HR, legal, or security concerns block any field activity logging.

### Discovery Call Script

Opening:

`Thanks for taking the time. I want to understand how your outside-sales team works before talking about product details. Prometheus Route IQ is most useful when there is a real field execution problem: reps need better direction, managers need clearer visibility, and leadership needs proof that field time is producing.`

Field motion:

- Who goes into the field?
- What are they trying to sell, book, inspect, quote, generate, or influence?
- How many reps, managers, locations, or markets are involved?
- How does a rep decide where to go today?
- What happens before an opportunity reaches CRM?

Pain and visibility:

- Where do reps lose the most time?
- What activity is hard for managers to verify?
- Which reports are trusted today?
- Which reports are ignored?
- What do managers wish they knew by the end of each field day?

Operating Profile:

- Which industry language fits your team best: Fitness, Roofing, Security, Medical, Insurance, or General Outside Sales?
- What does your team call a successful field outcome?
- What outcomes should be tracked during a pilot?
- What data should not be entered?

Pilot fit:

- Who would approve a controlled pilot?
- Who would manage daily adoption?
- Which team or location is safest for the first pilot?
- What would make the pilot worth expanding?
- If the pilot works, what paid rollout path is realistic?

Close:

`Based on what you shared, I would recommend either a controlled pilot design or a no-fit decision. If we move forward, the next step is a written pilot scope with users, dates, Operating Profile, success criteria, review cadence, and data boundaries.`

### Fitness Connection-Style Enterprise Pilot Motion

Use this motion when selling to a multi-location organization that resembles Fitness Connection: field reps, local outreach, manager visibility needs, measurable units or leads, and a path from one location to a regional rollout.

Phase 1: executive permission.

- Ask for a controlled pilot, not a full rollout.
- Keep the pilot narrow enough to reduce operational risk.
- Identify the executive sponsor, operating manager, and pilot users.
- Confirm legal, HR, privacy, and data expectations before launch.

Phase 2: location-level validation.

- Select one location, market, district, or team.
- Define the Operating Profile and field outcomes.
- Train reps and managers.
- Run the pilot for 14 to 30 days.
- Review results weekly.

Phase 3: pilot result review.

- Compare adoption, activity, outcomes, follow-up, manager visibility, and route quality against baseline.
- Separate verified results from assumptions.
- Recommend stop, extend, expand, or paid deployment.

Phase 4: multi-location expansion.

- Expand only after the first team shows adoption and manager value.
- Add locations in batches.
- Standardize onboarding, scorecards, reporting, support, and renewal metrics.

Buyer-facing ask:

`Approve a controlled pilot with a small group of approved users, weekly review, defined success criteria, and a final executive readout. If the pilot proves value, we can discuss a larger paid deployment by location, market, or region.`

### ROI And Value Framework

Prometheus value should be discussed through measurable operating improvement:

- More productive field visits.
- Less wasted driving or territory time.
- Better follow-up completion.
- More qualified outcomes.
- Faster rep ramp.
- Better manager coaching.
- Clearer executive visibility.
- Stronger expansion decisions.

ROI model:

`pilot value = incremental qualified outcomes + time saved + follow-up recovered + manager visibility value - pilot cost`

Required buyer inputs:

- Field users in pilot.
- Field days per month.
- Average field visits per day.
- Current qualified outcomes per field day.
- Value of one qualified outcome.
- Rep cost per field day.
- Current follow-up completion rate.
- Manager time spent chasing activity reports.

Buyer-facing ROI language:

`The pilot should not depend on inflated revenue promises. It should measure whether Prometheus improves the leading indicators that create revenue: better visits, better follow-up, more qualified outcomes, and clearer management decisions.`

### Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| We already have CRM. | Prometheus is not replacing CRM. It improves the field motion that creates CRM-worthy opportunities. |
| We do not want reps tracked. | Prometheus is built for intentional visit verification, not background surveillance. The pilot defines what is logged and who reviews it. |
| We need integrations first. | If integration is required for safety, we should pause. If not, the first pilot should prove field value before adding integration complexity. |
| Our industry is different. | Operating Profiles adapt language and success outcomes while preserving the same outside-sales operating loop. |
| We cannot run a large rollout. | The ask is a controlled pilot with approved users, scope, dates, and success criteria. |
| We are not sure about ROI. | The pilot exists to collect adoption, activity, and outcome evidence before a paid rollout decision. |
| Reps will not log activity. | Pilot design should use minimal required fields and prove whether the workflow is usable in real field conditions. |
| This sounds like route planning. | Route planning gives directions. Prometheus connects route decisions to outcomes, follow-up, learning, and manager visibility. |

### Pricing Guidance

Pricing should follow proof and scope.

Pilot pricing options:

- Design partner pilot: limited or discounted only when the customer provides executive access, structured feedback, and a paid rollout path.
- Paid pilot: recommended for enterprise or multi-location prospects when onboarding, support, and reporting are meaningful.
- Founding partner pricing: appropriate when the customer can become the first strong multi-location proof case.

Reference ranges:

- Small controlled pilot: $500 to $2,500 setup, then $49 to $79 per user per month if paid.
- Regional or multi-location pilot: $2,500 to $7,500 setup, then $49 to $99 per user per month.
- Enterprise rollout: $2,500 to $15,000 setup depending on scope, with per-user or enterprise agreement pricing.

Pricing rule:

Do not lead with price. Lead with the pilot business case, the operational problem, the users, the support load, and the decision after pilot evidence.

### Pilot Success Criteria

Minimum success:

- Approved users participate.
- At least 70% of intended field visits are logged.
- Reps can use the workflow without excessive admin friction.
- Managers receive clearer visibility than before the pilot.
- At least one measurable field outcome is captured.
- No unresolved privacy, HR, legal, or operational blocker appears.

Strong success:

- At least 85% of intended field visits are logged.
- Managers review Prometheus data weekly.
- Follow-up capture improves.
- Productive locations, categories, territories, or time windows are identified.
- The customer can explain what changed operationally.
- A larger pilot or paid rollout is justified.

Exceptional success:

- Usage becomes part of the operating rhythm.
- Outcome improvement is visible against baseline.
- Managers can make route, coaching, or territory decisions from the data.
- The executive sponsor requests expansion.
- A paid deployment path is clear.

### Post-Pilot Expansion Path

Expansion should follow proof:

1. Same team, more users.
2. Same location, second manager or adjacent team.
3. Same market, additional locations.
4. Regional rollout with standardized onboarding.
5. National or enterprise deployment with procurement, legal, security, reporting, and renewal governance.

Expansion readiness checklist:

- Adoption is stable.
- Field outcomes are measurable.
- Manager review is active.
- Support load is manageable.
- Buyer can name the next location or team.
- Pricing and contract path are realistic.
- Required legal/security/procurement steps are known.

## Founder-Facing Operating Guide

### Founder Sales Sequence

1. Start with discovery, not demo.
2. Confirm Operating Profile fit.
3. Identify executive sponsor and operating owner.
4. Qualify the pilot against the seven criteria.
5. Propose a written pilot scope.
6. Run a focused demo around the buyer's field motion.
7. Discuss ROI only through verified or buyer-provided inputs.
8. Close for pilot approval, not vague interest.
9. Review weekly.
10. Convert post-pilot evidence into stop, extend, expand, or paid deployment.

### Founder Discovery Control

Do not continue if the buyer cannot answer:

- What outcome should the field team produce?
- Who will ensure users actually use the pilot?
- Who reviews the results?
- What would make the pilot worth expanding?
- What systems or data must not be touched?

### Founder Demo Control

Demo only these concepts unless the buyer has requested more:

- Field direction.
- Visit logging.
- Operating Profile language.
- Manager visibility.
- Outcome learning.
- Pilot scorecard.
- Expansion path.

Do not show:

- Companion.
- Internal source code.
- Proprietary scoring logic.
- Private pilot data.
- Unsupported integration behavior.
- Product areas still under review.

### Founder Pricing Control

Use price only after value is clear.

Default founder language:

`If the pilot is narrow and mostly about validation, we can structure it as a design-partner pilot. If the pilot requires onboarding, reporting, support, and executive review, it should be a paid pilot. If the pilot proves adoption and measurable field value, the next step is a paid deployment by user, location, market, or region.`

### Founder Post-Pilot Close

Use this close after results are reviewed:

`The pilot showed [adoption], [field activity], [manager visibility], and [measurable outcome]. The recommended next step is [deployment scope] for [users/locations] over [term], with success measured by [renewal metrics].`

If results are weak:

`The pilot did not yet justify expansion. The honest recommendation is to stop, extend with a narrower scope, or resolve the blocker before discussing paid rollout.`

- Expansion: territory -> branch -> market -> regional rollout.

- Buyer: owner, VP sales, branch manager, commercial sales leader.
- Pilot outcome: site surveys, quotes, contracts, qualified leads.
- Pilot motion: residential or commercial team for 30 days.
- ROI: more site assessments, stronger property targeting, better follow-up.
- Expansion: team -> branch -> market -> enterprise accounts.

### Medical

- Buyer: CEO, COO, VP business development, director of physician relations.
- Pilot outcome: provider appointments, referrals, practice visits, agreements.
- Pilot motion: one market, service line, or referral-source team for 30 days.
- ROI: better referral-source coverage, relationship follow-up, and manager visibility.
- Expansion: service line -> market -> region -> enterprise referral operations.

- Buyer: agency owner, managing partner, sales manager, regional director.
- Pilot outcome: appointments, policies, referrals, employer meetings.

- Expansion: producer team -> agency -> region -> multi-office deployment.

### General Outside Sales

- Buyer: owner, VP sales, COO, field operations leader, regional director.
- Pilot outcome: qualified opportunities, appointments, contracts, sales, follow-ups.
- Pilot motion: one team, territory, or location for 30 days.
- ROI: higher outcome per field day, less wasted travel, better manager coaching.
- Expansion: team -> location -> market -> region -> enterprise.
