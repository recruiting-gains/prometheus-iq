# Roofing Discovery And Pilot Targeting

Purpose: give Prometheus Sales a roofing-specific package for evaluating, qualifying, and selling Prometheus IQ into roofing companies without guessing.

Roofing is the first recommended post-Fitness expansion vertical because the field motion is explicit: canvassing, storm response, territory coverage, inspections, estimates, follow-up, and signed contracts. Prometheus should enter roofing as an outside-sales intelligence layer, not as a CRM replacement, roofing production system, insurance-claim platform, or generic canvassing map.

## Sales Boundary

This package is Sales-owned. It does not modify or authorize changes to Companion, Website, CRM runtime, Mobile, Dashboard, Infrastructure, or Intelligence Engine.

Sales may use this package to run discovery, qualify pilot targets, prepare founder calls, write follow-up emails, shape pilot success criteria, and decide whether a roofing company is worth a controlled pilot.

## Target Customer Profile

Best-fit roofing companies:

- Residential or mixed residential/commercial roofing companies.
- 5 to 100 field sellers, canvassers, inspectors, storm reps, or neighborhood reps.
- Active door knocking, neighborhood canvassing, storm response, referral canvassing, or local business development.
- Clear conversion path from door/visit to inspection, estimate, and signed contract.
- Sales manager, canvassing manager, or owner who reviews field performance weekly.
- Territory waste, missed follow-up, or inconsistent canvasser accountability is a known problem.
- Uses CRM, spreadsheets, paper sheets, texts, maps, or canvassing tools but still lacks clear field intelligence.
- Has one branch, crew, market, neighborhood pod, or storm-response team that can run a controlled 14 to 30 day pilot.

Poor-fit roofing companies:

- Mostly inbound-only lead flow with no field prospecting.
- No manager accountable for canvasser behavior.
- No measurable inspection or estimate workflow.
- Wants Prometheus to replace production scheduling, insurance supplements, CRM, or job costing.
- Cannot define legal, privacy, HR, or field-conduct boundaries for canvassing.
- Has no credible paid rollout path after pilot proof.

Priority target segments:

1. Storm-response roofing teams that need fast territory learning.
2. Residential roofing companies using canvassers or neighborhood reps.
3. Multi-branch roofing companies with uneven field performance.
4. Commercial roofing teams using field account visits and property-manager outreach.
5. Roofing companies already paying for canvassing software but still lacking outcome intelligence.

## Roofing Value Proposition

Prometheus IQ helps roofing companies turn canvassing and field visits into measurable territory intelligence.

Buyer-facing version:

`Prometheus IQ helps roofing teams know which neighborhoods to work, verify field activity, track inspections and estimates, learn which territories produce contracts, and improve follow-up after every visit.`

Founder-facing version:

`Sell roofing on inspection and territory intelligence. Do not sell maps. Do not sell CRM replacement. The wedge is helping the roofing owner see whether field motion is creating inspections, estimates, and signed contracts instead of only doors knocked.`

Role-specific value:

- Owner: clearer ROI on canvassing labor, better market coverage, and a path from pilot proof to branch expansion.
- Sales manager: visibility into inspections, estimates, follow-up, rep performance, and territory productivity.
- Canvassing manager: cleaner accountability for doors/visits, neighborhoods worked, callback quality, and daily rep execution.
- Operations manager: better forecast of inspections and estimates that may become production load, without turning Sales into production scheduling.

## Roofing Discovery Questions

### Field Motion

1. How does your team currently generate roofing opportunities: canvassing, storm response, referrals, inbound, commercial outreach, or prior-customer revisits?
2. Who is in the field: canvassers, inspectors, sales reps, project managers, or hybrid roles?
3. How many field reps are active on a normal week?
4. How are neighborhoods, storm areas, or commercial territories assigned?
5. How does a rep decide which street, subdivision, or property to visit next?

### Inspection And Estimate Workflow

1. What counts as a qualified inspection?
2. What percentage of field conversations become inspections?
3. What percentage of inspections become estimates?
4. What percentage of estimates become signed contracts?
5. Where do opportunities most often drop: door conversation, inspection booking, estimate follow-up, insurance/claim step, or contract close?

### Territory Coverage

1. Which neighborhoods or property types usually produce the best jobs?
2. How do you know when a territory has been worked enough?
3. How do you prevent reps from reworking weak areas while missing stronger areas?
4. How do storm events change territory priorities?
5. What information does a manager need by the end of each field day?

### Canvasser Accountability

1. How do managers verify that canvassers worked assigned territory?
2. What proof do you currently trust: texts, photos, CRM updates, door counts, GPS, or manager ride-alongs?
3. What field activity is overreported or underreported?
4. What is the minimum logging workflow reps would actually complete?
5. What behavior would make you stop a pilot?

### Tools And Reporting

1. What CRM, canvassing app, spreadsheet, map tool, or job system do you use today?
2. What does your current system handle well?
3. What does it fail to show about field activity?
4. How do you compare canvassers, neighborhoods, inspections, estimates, and contracts?
5. Which reports do managers actually review weekly?

### Pilot Fit

1. Which team, territory, or branch is safest for a controlled pilot?
2. Who would approve the pilot?
3. Who would manage daily adoption?
4. What outcomes should the pilot prove first?
5. If Prometheus improves inspection volume, estimate follow-up, or territory coverage, what paid rollout path is realistic?

## Roofing Pilot Qualification Criteria

A roofing pilot is qualified when all criteria are true:

1. The company has active field prospecting or territory-based sales.
2. The company can measure at least two of: inspections, estimates, signed contracts, territory coverage, canvasser accountability.
3. A sales manager, canvassing manager, or owner will review pilot data weekly.
4. The pilot can be scoped to one team, territory, branch, or storm-response group.
5. The customer agrees Prometheus will not replace CRM, job production, supplements, or insurance-claim management during the pilot.
6. The company can define what field data should and should not be entered.
7. A successful pilot can lead to a paid deployment by user, branch, market, or team.

Score each criterion:

- 0: missing.
- 1: partially true.
- 2: verified.

Qualification bands:

- 12 to 14: propose a controlled pilot.
- 8 to 11: continue discovery before proposal.
- 4 to 7: nurture or research only.
- 0 to 3: disqualify.

## Pilot Design

Recommended pilot:

- Duration: 14 to 30 days.
- Users: 3 to 8 field reps or canvassers.
- Scope: one branch, territory, storm-response group, or canvassing pod.
- Manager: one accountable sales or canvassing manager.
- Review cadence: weekly, plus final pilot readout.
- Data boundaries: no private homeowner details beyond approved pilot fields.

Daily workflow:

1. Rep reviews assigned territory or route.
2. Rep works approved area.
3. Rep logs meaningful visits or door conversations.
4. Rep records inspection, estimate, follow-up, no interest, no decision maker, or revisit.
5. Manager reviews field activity and outcomes.
6. Team adjusts territory, revisit, or follow-up plan.

## Pilot Success Metrics

Adoption:

- Active pilot users.
- Field days logged.
- Percentage of intended field visits logged.
- Rep workflow friction.

Activity:

- Doors or property visits logged.
- Decision makers reached.
- Conversations.
- Follow-ups created.
- Follow-ups completed.

Revenue path:

- Inspections booked.
- Estimates scheduled or sent.
- Signed contracts.
- Contract value influenced, if approved by customer.

Territory:

- Neighborhoods or territories covered.
- Revisit opportunities identified.
- Productive neighborhoods found.
- Low-yield areas deprioritized.

Accountability:

- Verified visits where appropriate.
- Manager confidence in activity reporting.
- Reduction in end-of-day ambiguity.
- Coaching actions taken from pilot data.

Minimum success:

- 70% of intended field activity is logged.
- At least one measurable outcome is captured: inspection, estimate, contract, or follow-up improvement.
- Manager reports clearer visibility than before pilot.

Strong success:

- 85% of intended field activity is logged.
- Inspection or estimate flow improves against baseline or becomes visibly easier to manage.
- Productive territories or revisit patterns are identified.
- Manager uses Prometheus data for weekly coaching.

Exceptional success:

- Pilot creates a clear business case for branch, team, or market expansion.
- Owner can connect Prometheus to canvassing ROI, inspection pipeline, or contract creation.

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| We already use a canvassing app. | That may handle maps and activity capture. Prometheus focuses on field intelligence: which territories create inspections, estimates, contracts, and better follow-up. |
| Storm work changes too fast. | Fast-changing territory is exactly where daily field learning matters. The pilot should test whether Prometheus helps managers adjust faster. |
| Our reps hate admin. | The pilot should use minimal required fields tied to roofing outcomes: visit, inspection, estimate, follow-up, contract, or no fit. |
| We do not want to track people. | Prometheus should verify intentional field work, not run background surveillance. The pilot defines when verification is used and who reviews it. |
| Roofing is relationship-based. | Prometheus does not replace relationships. It helps the team invest field time where relationship work is most likely to create inspections and contracts. |
| We need CRM integration. | Integration may matter later. The first pilot should prove whether Prometheus improves territory execution before adding integration complexity. |
| Door knocking is unpredictable. | Prometheus helps identify which neighborhoods, timing, and follow-up patterns are less random and more repeatable. |
| We only care about signed contracts. | Contracts matter, but inspections and estimates are the leading indicators that show whether field activity is creating contract opportunity. |

## First-Call Script

Opening:

`I am reaching out because Prometheus IQ is being evaluated for roofing teams that rely on canvassing, inspections, estimates, and territory coverage. I am not calling to replace your CRM or production system. I want to understand whether your field team has a territory-intelligence problem worth piloting.`

Problem framing:

`Most roofing companies can count doors, inspections, estimates, and contracts after the fact. The harder question is whether the team knows which territories, reps, time windows, and follow-ups are actually creating those outcomes.`

Discovery:

1. `How do your reps decide which neighborhoods or properties to work each day?`
2. `What is the most important field outcome right now: inspections, estimates, signed contracts, or territory coverage?`
3. `How do managers verify canvasser activity today?`
4. `Where do you lose the most opportunity: door conversations, inspection booking, estimate follow-up, or contract close?`
5. `If a 14 to 30 day pilot showed better visibility into inspections, estimates, and territory productivity, who would need to review the results?`

Pilot close:

`If there is a fit, the next step would be a controlled pilot with one team or territory, approved users, clear success metrics, weekly review, and no CRM or production-system replacement. The goal would be to prove whether Prometheus improves field execution before discussing a larger rollout.`

Disqualification close:

`If your team is mostly inbound or you already have clean visibility from visit to contract, Prometheus may not be the right pilot right now. The fit is strongest where field territory decisions are still too manual or unclear.`

## Pilot Targeting Checklist

Before targeting a roofing company, confirm:

- They have active canvassing, storm response, territory sales, or commercial field outreach.
- They care about inspections, estimates, contracts, territory coverage, or canvasser accountability.
- They have at least one manager who reviews field activity.
- Their current tool stack leaves a visibility or learning gap.
- They can pilot without integrations.
- They can define approved field data.
- They have a realistic paid rollout path if the pilot works.

High-priority outreach signal:

- Hiring canvassers or sales reps.
- Expanding into a new market.
- Advertising storm restoration services.
- Posting about recent hail/wind events.
- Publicly emphasizing inspections, estimates, or free roof assessments.
- Multi-location branch structure.
- Active social proof showing field teams or neighborhood outreach.

Avoid or deprioritize:

- Companies with only subcontracted lead buying and no field team.
- Companies focused entirely on production operations.
- Companies with unclear reputation or aggressive field practices.
- Companies requiring insurance-claim automation as the primary value.
