# Discovery And Qualification

Purpose: provide reusable discovery and qualification systems for any outside-sales industry.

## Discovery Objective

Discovery should identify whether the customer has a costly field-sales execution gap that Prometheus can measure and improve.

The goal is not to collect interesting background. The goal is to determine:

- Whether field activity is important.
- Whether field activity is inefficient, invisible, or inconsistent.
- Whether outcomes can be measured.
- Whether a controlled pilot can prove value.
- Whether a paid rollout path exists.

## Discovery Framework

### 1. Field Motion

Questions:

- Who goes into the field?
- What are they trying to sell, book, inspect, collect, quote, or generate?
- How many reps operate in the field today?
- How many field days happen per week?
- What territory, radius, or market do reps cover?
- What happens before a lead reaches the CRM?

### 2. Daily Execution

Questions:

- How does a rep decide where to go today?
- What does a productive day look like?
- What actions must happen before the outcome is created?
- What time windows matter?
- What types of locations or accounts are usually worth visiting?
- What causes reps to waste time or drive without results?

### 3. Management Visibility

Questions:

- How do managers know where reps went?
- How do managers know whether the visit was useful?
- What does leadership review daily, weekly, and monthly?
- Where are reporting gaps?
- What decisions are made from field data today?
- Which reports do managers distrust or ignore?

### 4. Existing Stack

Questions:

- What CRM, route planner, mapping tool, spreadsheet, or reporting system is used now?
- What does the CRM handle well?
- What does it not capture before a lead exists?
- Are reps using phone notes, texts, spreadsheets, or memory?
- What systems must Prometheus avoid replacing during a pilot?

### 5. Metrics And Economics

Questions:

- What are the most important field-sales outputs?
- What is one appointment, inspection, unit, deal, claim, demo, or qualified lead worth?
- What is the cost of a rep day?
- What is the cost of wasted mileage, missed routes, or low activity quality?
- What conversion rates are known?
- What improvement would justify a paid deployment?

### 6. Pilot Readiness

Questions:

- Who can approve a pilot?
- Who owns daily adoption?
- Who reviews pilot results?
- How many users should be included?
- What data may be entered?
- What privacy, HR, legal, or security concerns need review?
- What outcome would make the pilot worth expanding?

## Universal Discovery Questions

Use these questions when time is limited:

1. What does your outside sales team need to produce in the field?
2. How do reps decide where to go today?
3. Where do reps lose the most time, fuel, or opportunity?
4. How do managers verify useful field activity?
5. What does your CRM fail to show before a lead or opportunity exists?
6. Which field metrics matter most to leadership?
7. What result would make a 14 to 30 day pilot successful?
8. Who must approve, operate, and review the pilot?

## Qualification Framework

Score each opportunity from 0 to 2 in each category.

0 means missing or weak.
1 means present but uncertain.
2 means strong and verified.

| Category | What To Verify | Disqualifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field motion | Outside reps perform recurring field activity | No outside sales activity |
| Pain | Route waste, low visibility, inconsistent execution, or poor learning | Prospect only wants generic CRM |
| Measurable outcome | Clear field output and baseline | No way to measure improvement |
| Buyer | Economic owner or sponsor exists | No authority path |
| Operator | Manager can enforce adoption | No daily owner |
| Pilot scope | Users, timeline, data boundaries, and review cadence can be defined | Prospect wants vague trial |
| Deployment path | Successful pilot can lead to paid rollout | No budget or expansion path |
| Risk review | Privacy, HR, legal, and security concerns can be handled | Risk blocks any field data use |

Qualification bands:

- 13 to 16: qualified for pilot design.
- 9 to 12: continue discovery before demo or pilot.
- 5 to 8: nurture or research only.
- 0 to 4: disqualify.

## Handoff To Demo

Before a demo, prepare:

- Customer-specific value hypothesis.
- Industry language map.
- One rep workflow.
- One manager workflow.
- Three success metrics.
- Known objections.
- Clear pilot ask.

Do not create or request a customer-specific demo dashboard unless discovery shows a credible pilot path.
