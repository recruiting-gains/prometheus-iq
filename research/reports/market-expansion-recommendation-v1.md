# Founder Strategic Market Expansion Recommendation v1

Date: 2026-07-02

Subsystem: Prometheus Research

Status: Founder-ready strategic research report

Recommendation: Expand first into Roofing.

## Executive Recommendation

Prometheus should make Roofing the first post-Fitness expansion market.

Roofing is the strongest next market because it combines high outside-sales intensity, frequent field visits, territory-based canvassing, inspection-driven workflows, large revenue opportunity, clear pain around wasted motion, and relatively straightforward Operating Profile translation. Solar is close, but policy volatility, financing sensitivity, and heavier competitive saturation make it a better second expansion target. HVAC is third because it has strong route and service density, but its sales motion is more technician/service-dispatch adjacent than Roofing's direct outside-sales motion.

## Method

This report evaluates markets using the Market Intelligence Engine v1 model and the requested expansion criteria:

- Market size
- Outside-sales workforce
- Frequency of field visits
- Territory-based selling
- Revenue opportunity
- AI adoption potential
- Ease of onboarding with Operating Profiles
- Sales cycle complexity
- Expansion potential

Scores are research judgments on a 0-100 scale. They are not production recommendations and do not modify Companion, Dashboard, CRM, Mobile, Website, or Intelligence runtime behavior.

## Why #1 Outranks #2

Roofing outranks Solar because Roofing has a cleaner first expansion path for Prometheus. The core workflow is easy to model: territory, doors, storm/weather context, inspections, estimates, follow-up, crew handoff, and close. Solar has similar field-sales intensity, but its go-to-market is more exposed to financing conditions, policy changes, utility rules, and an already crowded canvassing stack. Roofing gives Prometheus a sharper chance to prove field intelligence without becoming dependent on external policy incentives.

## Why #2 Outranks #3

Solar outranks HVAC because Solar is more directly outside-sales led. It depends heavily on canvassing, territory design, lead creation before CRM, appointment setting, and rep-level field execution. HVAC is a strong market, but much of the field motion is service-technician dispatch, maintenance, replacement quoting, and emergency response. Prometheus can serve HVAC, but Solar maps more directly to the current Prometheus promise: where should a rep go next, why, and what should happen after the visit?

These markets have strong route/territory pressure, clear field activity, identifiable reps or canvassers, and Operating Profile patterns Prometheus can model quickly.

## Industry Evaluations

### 1. Roofing

Opportunity score: 86

Outside-sales fit: Very high. Roofing depends on canvassing, storm response, territory coverage, inspections, estimates, and follow-up.

Operating Profile complexity: Low to medium. Core metrics can map cleanly to doors knocked, inspections booked, estimates, claims, follow-ups, contracts, and revisit windows.

TAM observations: Public IBISWorld summaries estimate U.S. roofing contractors at roughly a $92.5B market with more than 109,000 businesses in 2026. BLS/O*NET data also supports a large physical workforce around roofing activity.

AI differentiation opportunity: High. Prometheus can differentiate by recommending neighborhoods, revisits, storm-affected areas, inspection sequences, best canvassing windows, and rep/category fit without pretending to be a generic CRM.

Competitive landscape: SalesRabbit and other canvassing tools already serve roofing, so Prometheus must position around field intelligence and learning, not basic door-knocking maps.

Recommended priority: Immediate target, #1.

Risks:

- Crowded canvassing software category.
- Weather/event-driven demand can distort normal sales patterns.
- Insurance-claim workflows introduce operational complexity.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Roofing is the best first expansion market because the field-sales motion is explicit, measurable, painful, and repeatable.

### 2. Solar

Opportunity score: 83

Outside-sales fit: Very high. Residential solar sales frequently uses canvassing, appointment setting, territory coverage, and field qualification.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. Metrics can map to doors knocked, qualified homes, consultations booked, proposals, signed deals, install handoff, and financing status.

TAM observations: SEIA reports the U.S. has hundreds of gigawatts of installed solar capacity and millions of solar systems. IREC reports roughly 280,000 solar jobs in the United States, showing a substantial labor ecosystem. SEIA also reports recent market contraction, which is an important risk signal.

AI differentiation opportunity: High. Prometheus can improve territory targeting, route timing, qualification sequencing, follow-up discipline, and rep-specific field learning.

Competitive landscape: Solar is already served by canvassing and field-sales platforms. Prometheus must compete on explainable intelligence and customer-specific learning.

Recommended priority: Immediate target, #2.

Risks:

- Policy and incentive volatility.
- Financing sensitivity.
- Crowded door-to-door software landscape.
- Reputational risk from aggressive canvassing practices in parts of the market.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Solar is a strong second expansion target because its sales motion is highly aligned with Prometheus, but market volatility makes it slightly less attractive than Roofing as the first move.

### 3. HVAC

Opportunity score: 82

Outside-sales fit: High. HVAC combines local service territories, estimates, maintenance contracts, replacement sales, and business development.

Operating Profile complexity: Low to medium. Metrics can map to inspections, estimates, service calls, maintenance agreements, replacements, follow-ups, and booked jobs.

TAM observations: BLS projects HVAC mechanics and installers to grow faster than average and lists tens of thousands of annual openings. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies shows very large annual home improvement and repair spending, which supports a broad home-service demand base.

AI differentiation opportunity: High. Prometheus can help rank neighborhoods, revisit prior estimates, identify dense service areas, and prioritize replacement opportunities.

Competitive landscape: HVAC has strong field-service software competition. Prometheus should avoid becoming dispatch software and instead focus on outside-sales intelligence.

Recommended priority: Immediate target, #3.

Risks:

- Service operations may dominate buyer attention.
- Existing field-service platforms already own scheduling, dispatch, and job management.
- Sales motions vary between residential replacement, commercial service, and maintenance contracts.

Why Prometheus should enter:

HVAC is attractive because local service density and high-value replacement opportunities create clear intelligence use cases, but Prometheus should enter through sales/estimate intelligence rather than technician dispatch.

### 4. Pest Control

Opportunity score: 78

Outside-sales fit: High. Pest control has recurring routes, neighborhood density, door-to-door selling in some segments, callbacks, inspections, and subscription plans.

Operating Profile complexity: Low. Metrics can map to doors knocked, inspections, recurring plans, callbacks, treatments, and renewals.

TAM observations: Public IBISWorld summaries place U.S. pest control services in the tens of billions with tens of thousands of businesses. BLS/O*NET data shows a meaningful field workforce for pest control workers.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium to high. Prometheus can improve territory density, revisit cadence, seasonal targeting, service-plan follow-up, and route efficiency.

Competitive landscape: Field-service and pest-specific platforms exist, but many focus on operations rather than pre-lead outside-sales intelligence.

Recommended priority: Immediate target, #4.

Risks:

- Smaller revenue ceiling than Roofing, Solar, HVAC, or Insurance.
- Some companies may prioritize route optimization for service technicians over sales.
- Seasonal demand variation.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Pest Control is a strong early vertical because it is operationally simple, route-dense, and recurring, making it a good proof market after Roofing/Solar/HVAC.

### 5. Home Services

Opportunity score: 77

Outside-sales fit: Medium to high. Home services includes many door-to-door, estimate-driven, and local territory sales motions.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. The category is broad, so Prometheus should not enter "home services" generically. It should use this as a portfolio category and select narrow wedges.

TAM observations: Harvard JCHS shows very large home improvement and repair spending, supporting a large demand base for local service businesses.

AI differentiation opportunity: High if narrowed. Prometheus can create strong value in estimate routing, revisit timing, neighborhood targeting, and rep follow-up discipline.

Competitive landscape: Highly fragmented, with field-service management, lead-generation, and CRM tools competing for buyer attention.

Recommended priority: Future target.

Risks:

- Too broad as an entry category.
- Buyer needs differ sharply across trades.
- Risk of becoming generic local-service software.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Prometheus should treat Home Services as a long-term expansion umbrella, not the first post-Fitness vertical. Roofing, HVAC, Pest Control, and Security are better first wedges inside this broader category.

### 6. Security

Opportunity score: 75

Outside-sales fit: High. Security sales involves canvassing, site surveys, local territory coverage, commercial/residential visits, and recurring revenue.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. Metrics can map to site surveys, qualified properties, consultations, proposals, installs, and recurring contract value.

TAM observations: Public IBISWorld summaries show sizable U.S. security system services and alarm monitoring markets. BLS/O*NET data supports a field installation and service workforce.

AI differentiation opportunity: High. Prometheus can help prioritize properties, revisit previous surveys, sequence territory coverage, and separate high-fit commercial/residential prospects.

Competitive landscape: Security has established dealer networks, CRMs, canvassing tools, and field-service platforms.

Recommended priority: Future target.

Risks:

- Compliance and consumer-protection concerns.
- Dealer/channel complexity.
- Existing door-to-door/security-specific tools.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Security fits Prometheus well, but it should come after Roofing/Solar/HVAC because sales practices and channel structures require more careful positioning.

### 7. Medical

Opportunity score: 72

Outside-sales fit: High in medical device and healthcare field sales. Reps visit clinics, hospitals, physicians, practices, and accounts.

Operating Profile complexity: High. Metrics require careful handling: meetings, referrals, product education, clinical access, compliance limits, account stage, and relationship depth.

TAM observations: AdvaMed reports a large U.S. medtech economic footprint and substantial employment. The market has high contract values and complex enterprise sales.

AI differentiation opportunity: High but compliance-limited. Prometheus could improve account routing, physician/practice visit planning, territory coverage, follow-up discipline, and rep coaching.

Competitive landscape: Enterprise CRM, life-sciences CRM, compliance systems, and territory management tools are entrenched.

Recommended priority: Future target.

Risks:

- Compliance and privacy constraints.
- Longer enterprise sales cycles.
- Need for strong governance before customer-facing claims.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Medical is a high-upside strategic target, but not the first expansion move. Prometheus should enter after proving repeatable value in less regulated outside-sales markets.

### 8. Insurance

Opportunity score: 70

Outside-sales fit: Medium to high. Insurance has a large agent base, local relationship selling, appointments, territory development, and referral networks.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. Metrics can map to appointments, policies, referrals, renewals, quotes, households, and commercial accounts.

TAM observations: NAIC and Insurance Information Institute data show a very large U.S. insurance market. O*NET/BLS data shows a large insurance sales agent workforce.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium. Prometheus can help field agents prioritize neighborhoods, local businesses, referrals, renewals, and follow-up.

Competitive landscape: CRM, agency management systems, lead vendors, and carrier platforms are established.

Recommended priority: Future target.

Risks:

- Heavier compliance and licensing.
- Sales may be appointment/referral-led rather than route-led.
- Many agents already live inside carrier or agency systems.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Insurance is attractive because of market size and agent count, but Prometheus should wait until it can prove a sharper field-intelligence wedge.

### 9. Commercial Services

Opportunity score: 68

Outside-sales fit: Medium to high. Commercial cleaning, facilities, landscaping, waste, and B2B local services depend on territory prospecting and site visits.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. Metrics can map to site visits, walkthroughs, quotes, contracts, renewals, and account density.

TAM observations: Public IBISWorld summaries show a large janitorial services market with many businesses. BLS data shows a large building-cleaning workforce.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium. Prometheus can improve territory coverage, site prioritization, revisit timing, and proposal follow-up.

Competitive landscape: Fragmented local operators plus vertical field-service and CRM tools.

Recommended priority: Future target.

Risks:

- Buyers may be price-sensitive.
- Sales cycles can involve procurement and facility managers.
- Category is broad and needs a wedge.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Commercial Services is viable after stronger proof in more urgent residential/service-adjacent verticals.

### 10. Telecommunications

Opportunity score: 65

Outside-sales fit: High in door-to-door, local territory, and B2B telecom sales, but uneven across operators.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium to high. Metrics can map to doors, qualified addresses, appointments, installs, churn, and serviceability.

TAM observations: Public IBISWorld summaries show large U.S. telecom markets. Telecom also has a meaningful field installation/service workforce.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium to high. Prometheus can improve territory assignment, address qualification, field rep timing, and install follow-up.

Competitive landscape: Highly competitive and incumbent-heavy, with existing canvassing, telecom sales, and internal operator tools.

Recommended priority: Long-term expansion.

Risks:

- Incumbent concentration.
- Regulatory complexity.
- Churn-heavy sales environments.
- High integration expectations.

Why Prometheus should enter:

Telecommunications is strategically relevant but should wait until Prometheus has stronger enterprise proof and integration readiness.

### 11. Merchant Services

Opportunity score: 63

Outside-sales fit: Medium. Merchant services historically used SMB field sales, but many motions are now remote, partner-led, or digitally acquired.

Operating Profile complexity: Medium. Metrics can map to SMB visits, demos, applications, approvals, activations, transaction volume, and referrals.

TAM observations: Federal Reserve payments data shows massive card/payment volume, and UBS analysis highlights a large U.S. acquiring revenue pool.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium. Prometheus could help field reps identify dense SMB clusters, revisit timing, and vertical-fit routes.

Competitive landscape: Extremely crowded, commoditized, and dominated by processors, fintech platforms, ISOs, POS providers, and embedded payments.

Recommended priority: Long-term expansion.

Risks:

- Commoditized pricing.
- High competition.
- Less pure route dependence than roofing/solar/pest/HVAC.
- Partner/channel complexity.

Why Prometheus should not enter immediately:

Merchant Services has a large financial market but weaker first-wedge clarity for Prometheus.

### 12. B2B Field Sales

Opportunity score: 61

Outside-sales fit: High as a general category, but too broad to enter directly.

Operating Profile complexity: High unless segmented. B2B field sales varies by industrial, manufacturing, wholesale, payroll, equipment, logistics, and commercial services.

TAM observations: BLS/O*NET data shows large employment across sales representatives and managers, validating the broad outside-sales market.

AI differentiation opportunity: High if verticalized. Low if Prometheus tries to sell generic B2B field sales software.

Competitive landscape: CRMs, sales engagement tools, field-sales platforms, territory planning software, and analytics tools are all active.

Recommended priority: Long-term expansion category.

Risks:

- Too abstract for first expansion.
- Hard messaging.
- High incumbent CRM gravity.

Why Prometheus should not enter directly:

B2B Field Sales is the long-term horizontal category, not the next market wedge. Prometheus should win vertical proof first.

### 13. Fitness

Opportunity score: 58

Outside-sales fit: Medium. Fitness validates local outreach, partnership selling, resident/community events, and LSM workflows.

Operating Profile complexity: Low. Fitness is already closest to the current validation environment.

TAM observations: Health & Fitness Association reporting supports a large U.S. facility and member/user base, but local field-sales sophistication varies widely by chain and operator.

AI differentiation opportunity: Medium. Prometheus can help local gyms execute better LSM, but the category may not support the same urgency or contract size as roofing, solar, HVAC, or medical.

Competitive landscape: Fitness CRMs, club management systems, marketing tools, and local outreach practices exist, but direct route-intelligence competition is weaker.

Recommended priority: Validation baseline, not expansion priority.

Risks:

- Lower enterprise urgency.
- Narrower buyer budget.
- Risk of being perceived as a gym sales tool.

Why Prometheus should not enter as the next expansion:

Fitness should remain the proof environment. Prometheus must expand beyond it to protect the outside-sales operating-system thesis.
