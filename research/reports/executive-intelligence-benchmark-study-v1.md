## Executive Summary

This is not a copy exercise. The benchmark question is: What makes executives trust recommendations?

This benchmark study reviewed public methodology signals from McKinsey & Company, Bain & Company, Boston Consulting Group, Deloitte Strategy and M&A, corporate development practice, private equity due diligence, investment banking M&A advisory, and strategic consulting communication practice.

The core finding is that executives trust recommendations when four conditions are present:

1. The decision question is explicit.
2. The recommendation is answer-first and tied to a value thesis.
3. Evidence is organized around the few assumptions that would change the decision.
4. Risks, unknowns, confidence, and next verification steps are visible instead of hidden.

Prometheus should not imitate any firm's branding, slide style, proprietary templates, or copyrighted formats. Prometheus should adopt the underlying decision disciplines that repeatedly appear across high-quality executive decision-support organizations:

- Hypothesis-led problem framing.
- MECE issue decomposition.
- Investment-thesis or decision-thesis discipline.
- Outside-in evidence first, followed by owner-verified evidence when available.
- Value-creation and risk-driver mapping.
- Competing-option comparison.
- Explicit confidence and unknown registers.
- Red-team review before recommendation.
- Executive action path with next investigation.

Prometheus Executive Intelligence Packages should therefore evolve from "complete information packets" into "decision-readiness packets." The package should make the executive's decision safer, faster, and more specific.

## Decision Framework Comparison

### Common Pattern

The highest-quality decision-support organizations do not begin with data collection. They begin by defining the decision:

- What is the executive deciding?
- What options are on the table?
- What facts would change the decision?
- What assumptions must be true?
- What risks could make the recommendation wrong?
- What must happen next if the recommendation is accepted?

### McKinsey Pattern

Public McKinsey material emphasizes structured problem solving, decision trees, clear problem definition, and strategy under uncertainty. Its M&A blueprint material frames dealmaking around corporate strategy, self-assessment, market assessment, and boundary conditions.

Prometheus implication: every Executive Intelligence Package should begin with the executive decision question and a decision thesis, not a broad company profile.

### Bain Pattern

Public Bain material emphasizes investment-thesis discipline, integrated diligence, full-potential assessment, operational and tech diligence, value-creation plans, and execution readiness.

Prometheus implication: packages should connect "what is attractive" to "what would need to happen for the opportunity to become real."

### BCG Pattern

Public BCG material emphasizes strategic due diligence as an iterative process of raising and addressing critical financial, operational, cultural, market, and value-creation questions. BCG's public M&A and transformation materials also emphasize scenario planning, target screening, and value creation.

Prometheus implication: packages should include both a market-level view and a target-specific view, then stress-test the recommendation.

### Deloitte Pattern

Public Deloitte material emphasizes robust due diligence governance, risk magnitude, probability, mitigation, intrinsic value, transaction alternatives, synergy identification, and value realization.

Prometheus implication: packages should treat risk as decision data, not as a disclaimer.

### PE / Corp Dev / Investment Banking Pattern

Private equity diligence tests a going-in thesis against facts and value levers. Corporate development ties opportunities to strategy, alternatives, market conditions, and boundary constraints. Investment banking advisory organizes transaction rationale, valuation, options, counterparties, and risks.

Prometheus implication: packages should combine PE-style assumption testing, corp-dev strategic fit, and banking-style options comparison without becoming a financial pitch book.

## Risk Communication Comparison

High-quality organizations communicate risk with structure:

- Risk description.
- Evidence basis.
- Probability or likelihood.
- Magnitude or business impact.
- Mitigation path.
- Verification path.
- Decision implication.

Deloitte's due diligence governance framing is especially relevant for board-ready risk communication because it asks whether risks can be assessed, sized, mitigated, and responded to. Bain and BCG add a value-creation lens: risk matters because it can break the deal thesis or make the execution plan unrealistic.

Prometheus should present risk in a compact decision table:

| Risk | Evidence | Impact | Likelihood | Mitigation | Verification | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Executive Presentation Comparison

Executive communication patterns repeat across consulting and finance:

- Answer first.
- State the recommended decision.
- Explain why now.
- Show the few strongest reasons.
- Identify the evidence behind each reason.
- Surface risks and unknowns early.
- Provide backup detail in appendices.
- End with a specific action path.

The best executive summaries are not summaries of everything known. They are decision control panels.

Prometheus should require every Executive Intelligence Package to open with:

1. Decision question.
2. Recommended executive posture.
3. Confidence.
4. Top three reasons.
5. Top three risks.
6. Top three unknowns.
7. One fact that would most improve decision quality.
8. Recommended next action.

## Private Equity vs Consulting Comparison

Private equity diligence is optimized for underwriting a decision under time pressure. It asks:

- What is the investment thesis?
- What must be true?
- What is the full potential?
- What can break the value plan?
- What is achievable in the first 100 days?
- What price or terms compensate for risk?

Consulting decision support is optimized for executive alignment and organizational action. It asks:

- What problem are we solving?
- What are the options?
- What evidence supports the recommendation?
- What operating model or change path is required?
- How do leaders act on the recommendation?

Prometheus should take the PE discipline of assumption testing and value-lever proof, but use consulting-style executive communication to make the recommendation usable in conversation.

## Corporate Development Comparison

Corporate development teams are the closest benchmark for Prometheus Executive Intelligence because they prepare executives before outreach, partnership, investment, acquisition, or strategic relationship decisions.

The strongest public corp-dev patterns:

- Maintain an M&A or strategic opportunity blueprint.
- Define strategic fit before target excitement.
- Establish boundary conditions before outreach.
- Compare the opportunity against alternatives.
- Keep market conditions visible.
- Tie diligence to integration or partnership reality.
- Maintain a pipeline and update the thesis as new facts emerge.

Prometheus adoption:

- Use a "decision thesis" before every executive conversation.
- Record "why this company, why now, why us."
- Define what Cruz or JR should verify first.
- Compare against the current mission queue.
- Preserve post-meeting learning so future recommendations improve.

## What Makes Executives Trust Recommendations

Executives trust recommendations when the package demonstrates:

1. Judgment: the research team knows what matters and what does not.
2. Traceability: claims can be traced to sources or clearly marked assumptions.
3. Challenge: the recommendation was attacked before it was delivered.
4. Alternatives: the recommendation survived comparison against other options.
5. Candor: risks and unknowns are visible.
6. Decision specificity: the recommendation names what to do next.
7. Reversibility awareness: the package clarifies what can be learned before committing further.
8. Confidence calibration: confidence is not overstated.
9. Executive fit: the output is usable in a real conversation.

## Recommended Prometheus Executive Intelligence Methodology

Prometheus should adopt the following methodology:

Canonical methodology terms: decision framing, decision thesis, issue tree, evidence map, value and risk driver map, alternatives and opportunity cost, scenario and decision tree, unknown value ranking, red team review, executive brief and appendices.

### 1. Decision Framing

Define the exact executive decision:

- Call, do not call.
- Prioritize now, defer, or reject.
- Prepare JR for intro, founder discovery, or partnership exploration.
- Continue outside-in research or request owner-provided verification.

Why adopt: all benchmark organizations start with the decision, not the data pile.

Why not overdo it: early-stage outreach decisions should not be treated like full acquisition committee decisions.

### 2. Decision Thesis

Write one testable thesis:

Prometheus should speak with this business because [strategic reason], if [critical assumptions] are true, despite [main risks].

Why adopt: McKinsey M&A blueprint, Bain investment-thesis discipline, BCG strategic diligence, and corp-dev practice all rely on thesis clarity.

### 3. Issue Tree

Break the thesis into testable branches:

- Strategic fit.
- Business quality.
- Operator/ownership clarity.
- Expansion readiness.
- Ecosystem strength.
- Competitive position.
- Reputation and customer experience.
- Digital and marketing maturity.
- Conversation path.
- Risks and unknowns.

Why adopt: structured decomposition prevents unfocused research.

### 4. Evidence Map

Every material conclusion maps to:

- Evidence label.
- Source.
- Reliability.
- Strength.
- Contradiction.
- Verification needed.

Why adopt: executives trust what they can audit.

### 5. Value And Risk Driver Map

Map the business to drivers:

- Upside driver.
- Downside risk.
- Leading indicator.
- What would confirm.
- What would falsify.
- Conversation question.

Why adopt: PE and M&A advisory work converts research into value, risk, and action.

### 6. Alternatives And Opportunity Cost

Compare the target against known alternatives:

- Why this company before others?
- What is the cost of calling this business today?
- What stronger opportunity would displace it?

Why adopt: executives trust recommendations that survived comparison.

### 7. Scenario And Decision Tree

Prepare branches:

- If interest is high.
- If liquidity is uncertain.
- If they want one unit.
- If they want multiple units.
- If ownership is unclear.
- If timing is not right.
- If contradiction emerges.

Why adopt: this turns intelligence into conversation readiness.

### 8. Unknown Value Ranking

Rank unknowns by business value:

1. The one fact that most improves the decision.
2. The one risk that most threatens the thesis.
3. The one verification that determines the next action.

Why adopt: the best diligence teams do not just list unknowns; they prioritize them.

### 9. Red-Team Review

Attack the recommendation:

- What if we are wrong?
- What evidence is weakest?
- What are we assuming?
- What contradicts the thesis?
- What would change the recommendation?

Why adopt: decision quality improves when the case is challenged before the executive relies on it.

### 10. Executive Brief And Appendices

Package the output as:

- Answer-first executive brief.
- Decision logic.
- Evidence tables.
- Risk register.
- Unknown register.
- Source appendix.

Why adopt: executives need the answer fast and the audit trail nearby.
