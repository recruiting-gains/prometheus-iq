## Mandate

This package prepares the receiving Smoothie Depot executive to decide whether, where, and under what gates the franchise system should expand within Greater Houston.

It does not make the business decision. It does not execute outreach, negotiation, qualification, franchise sales, site selection, or capital allocation. It reduces uncertainty so the receiving executive can exercise those responsibilities with a stronger evidence base.

This is a new canonical package built from the current Research architecture. It is not a revision of a prior prospect dossier or package.

## Executive Decision Page

### Decision

Should Smoothie Depot accelerate Greater Houston franchise expansion now, and which expansion model should receive executive authorization?

### Decision thesis

**Recommendation: authorize a gated cluster-expansion program, not unrestricted metro-wide acceleration.**

Greater Houston presents a strong demand environment. The metro had an estimated 7.9 million residents in 2025, added approximately 126,720 residents in one year, and supports dense networks from scaled smoothie competitors. Smoothie Depot also has a real local base: its official site lists eight physical locations plus mobile service, with independently reported Katy drive-through and League City gym-based openings. [S05] [S08] [S09] [S22] [S29]

The constraint is system readiness, not market existence. The public record does not establish representative unit economics, current legal franchisor identity, audited franchisor capacity, outlet turnover, franchisee satisfaction, support throughput, or consistent operating quality. The official website also contains conflicting initial-investment totals. Public reviews show meaningful location-level divergence, especially in Webster. [S02] [S03] [S04] [S14] [S15] [S16]

The receiving executive should preserve the upside without accepting avoidable system risk. A 90-day proof sprint can validate or falsify the expansion case before more franchise rights, sites, or capital are committed.

### Recommended executive posture

Proceed as a disciplined system builder:

1. Establish the legal and disclosure foundation.
2. Prove representative economics across current formats.
3. Validate the installed franchisee base, including former operators.
4. Confirm that support capacity can absorb simultaneous openings.
5. Repair or explain the highest-risk operating and reputation variance.
6. Authorize two or three cluster pilots only after those gates pass.

### Overall confidence

| Question | Confidence |
| --- | --- |
| Is Greater Houston attractive for the category? | Medium-high to High |
| Does Smoothie Depot have a credible local operating base? | Medium-high |
| Is measured expansion plausible? | Medium |
| Is unrestricted expansion decision-ready today? | Low |
| Are the recommended proof gates the right immediate executive priority? | High |

### Immediate decision gates

| Gate | Evidence required | Pass condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Legal and disclosure | Current FDD, franchise agreement, legal entity, Texas filing, trademark chain | Authority is clear; public materials reconcile to controlled disclosures; counsel identifies no unresolved critical issue. |
| 2. Unit economics | Location-level sales and normalized P&Ls by format and age | More than one representative unit demonstrates durable economics without undisclosed owner-labor subsidy or exceptional rent. |
| 3. Outlet health | Item 20 history, current and former franchisee population, current outlet ledger | Openings, closures, transfers, and operator outcomes support the stated growth thesis. |
| 4. Support capacity | Organization chart, opening calendar, training and field-support service levels | The franchisor can support current units and two or three pilots without degradation. |
| 5. Operating consistency | Audit and recovery evidence for every current location, with emphasis on Webster | Material quality, hours, service, menu, and reputation gaps have owners, deadlines, and verified resolution. |
| 6. Site and operator underwriting | Trade-area model, territory rules, operator capability, prototype economics | Each pilot passes location, operator, cannibalization, support-radius, and return thresholds. |

### Opportunity Cost

If the receiving executive can authorize only one strategic initiative now, it should be the proof sprint.

Authorizing scattered expansion before economics and support capacity are verified can multiply legal exposure, franchisee dissatisfaction, inconsistent customer experience, and brand damage. Waiting indefinitely while only refining collateral would leave attractive Houston whitespace to better-capitalized competitors. The proof sprint is the highest-value middle path because it protects both options: it can accelerate expansion with controlled evidence, or expose a smaller and less costly remediation program before growth capital is committed.

**Evidence label:** `Inference`

**Evidence basis:** Attractive metro demand, a real local footprint, recent format expansion, missing controlled franchise evidence, an internal public-cost conflict, and reputation variance. [S03] [S05] [S08] [S09] [S14] [S15] [S22] [S23] [S29]

**Source reliability:** High for official and government facts; medium for review and readiness interpretation.

**Confidence level:** High that proof has greater expected decision value than immediate unrestricted expansion.

**Verification needed:** Determine whether complete internal evidence already closes the six gates. If it does, the proof period can be shortened.

**Business decision impact:** Concentrates executive attention on the information that most changes expansion value and risk.

## Layer 1: Executive Summary

### Decision context

Smoothie Depot has a credible opportunity to become a stronger Houston challenger. The brand has local history, eight publicly listed physical locations, recent format innovation, and an entry-cost proposition that appears materially below larger franchise comparators. Houston itself offers category demand, continued population growth, and multiple expansion corridors.

The decision is not whether smoothies can sell in Houston. The decision is whether Smoothie Depot can add units while preserving franchisee returns, customer consistency, disclosure quality, and support capacity.

### Executive recommendation

**Evidence label:** `Inference`

Authorize a 90-day proof sprint and condition all new cluster pilots on six gates: legal and disclosure, unit economics, outlet health, support capacity, operating consistency, and site/operator underwriting.

**Evidence basis:** Major Conclusions 1-7 and evidence items `SDV3-E001` through `SDV3-E027`.

**Source reliability:** Mixed by claim; High for official and government evidence, Medium for synthesis, and Low to Medium for public review and resale signals.

**Confidence level:** High in the gated process; Medium in the resulting expansion case until controlled evidence is reviewed.

**Verification needed:** Complete the six immediate gates.

**Business decision impact:** Enables expansion only where the franchise model, support system, operator, and site can jointly withstand executive diligence.

### Critical unknowns

1. Current FDD, legal franchisor, audited financials, and Texas offering status.
2. Representative unit economics by location, format, and age.
3. Openings, closures, transfers, reacquisitions, and franchisee outcomes.
4. Support-team capacity and simultaneous-opening throughput.
5. Current operating reality behind the Webster reputation divergence.
6. Territory rights, site criteria, and cannibalization controls.

### What would change the recommendation

The recommendation would become more aggressive if controlled evidence shows strong representative economics, low turnover, high franchisee satisfaction, clear authority, ample support capacity, and already-underwritten sites. It would become more conservative if the FDD or internal records show weak economics, churn, insufficient capital, unresolved compliance issues, or persistent location-quality failures.

## Red-Team Review

### What if this recommendation is wrong?

The package may overstate readiness risk because complete private evidence could already exist. A current FDD may establish clear legal authority, healthy Item 19 performance, low Item 20 turnover, strong Item 21 capacity, and satisfied franchisees. Internal data may show that Webster's public signals are stale or unrepresentative. Signed, time-sensitive sites may also create more value than a fixed 90-day diligence period.

If those conditions are true, the proof sprint should compress immediately. The recommendation is conditional, not a permanent slowdown.

### Weakest evidence

1. Public review platforms have different samples, dates, moderation, and possible authenticity issues.
2. The Pasadena resale listing is seller or broker supplied and cannot establish profitability or system health.
3. Corridor priorities rely on county growth and visible footprint rather than completed site underwriting.
4. Public search cannot prove that a valid FDD, Texas notice, capable support team, or healthy financials do not exist privately.
5. Social mirrors are directional and cannot support a reliable audience or engagement conclusion.

### Unsupported assumptions rejected

- The historical trademark application proves current company ownership.
- A franchise contact automatically has legal signing authority.
- Every officially listed location is currently active and franchised.
- A competitor's national AUV equals its Houston sales.
- High ratings prove strong unit economics.
- Low ratings prove system-wide failure.
- Population growth creates a viable site without underwriting.
- A lower initial investment automatically produces a better franchise return.

### What would prove the recommendation false?

- Controlled records already close the six immediate gates.
- Representative economics show strong returns across formats and age cohorts.
- Item 20 and franchisee validation show low churn and high reinvestment intent.
- Support capacity is already staffed for several simultaneous openings.
- Webster passes a current operating audit and its negative signals are stale or platform-specific.
- Two or more sites have independently underwritten returns whose time value exceeds the diligence delay.

### What evidence would make the recommendation more conservative?

- Weak or concentrated Item 19 performance.
- Multiple closures, transfers, or operator exits.
- Franchisees unwilling to reinvest.
- Audited financial weakness or inadequate support resources.
- Unresolved public-claim or offering-compliance issues.
- Persistent food, service, or management failures.
- Economics that require unpaid owner labor or exceptional rents.
