## Executive Summary

Prometheus began as a founder-built field-sales tool for solving a real outside-sales problem: field reps are often told to go produce results, but they are not given enough intelligence about where to go, when to go, what worked, what failed, or how their own strengths affect outcomes.

The company evolved from a gym-focused LSM tracker into Prometheus IQ: a field-sales intelligence product designed to guide field teams, verify activity, track outcomes, and convert daily field activity into better decisions over time.

By June 22, 2026, Prometheus had moved beyond an app prototype. It had:

- A live Cloudflare-hosted protected app.
- GPS pin-drop verification.
- Route Intelligence.
- Rep / Owner mode.
- Visit and outcome tracking.

- Security review.
- Executive pilot materials.
- Pilot approval package.
- Success metrics framework.
- Competitive differentiation.
- Pricing strategy.
- LLC launch plan.
- Founder operating system.
- Master Prometheus Operating System.
- Cinematic website work in progress.

The company is still early, but it now has the beginnings of a real operating structure.

## Lessons Learned

### What Worked

- Building from real field experience instead of abstract theory.
- Keeping the first niche focused on gyms and LSM activity.
- Creating a working app before chasing investors.
- Adding GPS verification to increase trust.
- Protecting the app with Cloudflare Access.
- Backing up source code to GitHub.
- Creating executive documents that reduce confusion.
- Separating sendable leadership materials from internal strategy.
- Practicing founder answers and sharpening the contrarian truth.
- Turning repeated conversations into documents and operating systems.

### What Did Not Work

- Too much information could overwhelm executives.
- Raw Markdown or code-style views are not good executive presentation formats.
- Too many features at once risked complexity for field users.
- Pricing discussions too early could distract from pilot approval.
- Public marketing could become risky if it revealed proprietary ideas.
- Relying only on the founder's memory would not scale.

### What Should Be Repeated

- Build from actual field pain.
- Keep the app simple for reps.
- Convert strategy into documents.
- Use pilots to prove outcomes.
- Keep sensitive materials internal.
- Create PDFs for executive review.
- Back up source code and app data.
- Run security reviews before expanding users.
- Use operating systems when the same task will happen again.

### What Should Be Avoided

- Overbuilding before pilot results.
- Revealing proprietary technology publicly.
- Sending too many documents to leadership.
- Making pricing promises before value is proven.
- Expanding into too many industries before the first pilot is validated.
- Making tired, late-night business commitments.
- Letting Prometheus depend entirely on founder memory.
