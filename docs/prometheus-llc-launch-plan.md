# Prometheus LLC Launch Plan

Purpose: Give Prometheus a clean operating foundation before paid pilots, business banking, domain ownership, and customer agreements begin.

Note: This is a practical operating plan, not legal or tax advice. Before signing paid agreements, taking investment, issuing equity, or making legal claims, review the documents with a Texas business attorney and CPA.

## 1. Exact Order Of Operations

Recommended sequence:

1. Finalize company name decision.
2. Search Texas Secretary of State name availability.
3. Search basic trademark conflicts.
4. Decide registered agent.
5. File Texas LLC Certificate of Formation.
6. Create company operating agreement.
7. Apply for EIN with IRS.
8. Open business bank account.
9. Create bookkeeping system.
10. Purchase company domain.
11. Create company email.
12. Move business subscriptions under company email.
13. Organize company records folder.
14. Prepare pilot agreement and consent documents under Prometheus LLC.
15. Review insurance needs before paid customer use.
16. Begin paid pilot discussions only after company banking and documents are ready.

## 2. What Should Happen Before LLC Filing

Before filing the Texas LLC:

- Confirm the legal name: Prometheus LLC or another available version.
- Confirm the product name: Prometheus IQ.
- Decide whether the public brand will be Prometheus and the product will be Prometheus IQ.
- Run a Texas entity name search.
- Run a basic trademark search for Prometheus in software / SaaS / sales categories.
- Decide who owns the LLC.
- Decide whether ownership is 100% founder-owned at formation.
- Decide the registered agent.
- Create a simple founder records folder.
- Keep receipts for domain, software, design, hosting, and app expenses.

Do not:

- Take investor money yet.
- Promise equity.
- Sign paid customer contracts in your personal name if the LLC is close to being formed.
- Publicly claim patent protection unless an attorney confirms the wording.
- Mix business money and personal money after the bank account is open.

## 3. What Should Happen Immediately After LLC Approval

Once the LLC is approved:

- Save the Certificate of Formation.
- Save the state filing receipt.
- Create and sign the operating agreement.
- Apply for EIN through the IRS.
- Create a company records folder.
- Open the business bank account.
- Start tracking all business income and expenses.
- Update customer-facing documents to use the correct legal entity name.
- Update pilot documents to say Prometheus LLC once confirmed.
- Purchase or transfer the domain under company control.
- Set up business email.
- Move Cloudflare, GitHub, Google, and other business tools toward company-controlled accounts where practical.

## 5. Business Banking Setup

Open the business bank account after EIN approval.

Recommended setup:

- Business checking account.
- Business debit card.
- Separate savings account for taxes.
- Optional business credit card later, only if used responsibly.

Bring or prepare:

- LLC approval documents.
- EIN confirmation.
- Operating Agreement.
- Founder ID.
- Business address.
- Business email.
- Phone number.

Rules:

- Do not mix personal and business funds.
- Pay Prometheus expenses from the business account once open.
- Deposit customer payments only into the business account.
- Keep receipts.
- Transfer owner draws clearly if money is taken out.

## 6. Accounting / Bookkeeping Setup

Start simple.

Recommended early setup:

- Use a spreadsheet at first if expenses are low.
- Move to QuickBooks, Wave, Xero, or another bookkeeping tool once payments begin.
- Track every expense by category.
- Save receipts monthly.
- Keep personal and business expenses separate.
- Review finances weekly during the first 90 days.

Basic categories:

- Hosting and cloud.
- Software subscriptions.
- Domain and email.
- Legal.
- Accounting.
- Marketing.
- Design.
- Travel / mileage.
- Equipment.
- Contractor payments.
- Customer revenue.

CPA questions to ask:

- How should Prometheus LLC be taxed initially?
- Should the LLC remain single-member disregarded entity at first?
- When would S-Corp election make sense?
- How should founder expenses before LLC formation be recorded?
- How should mileage and home office be handled?
- How should future investor money be handled?

## 8. Domain Setup

Domain purchase should happen after the name decision is final.

Recommended domain priorities:

1. Professional and credible.
2. Easy to say out loud.
3. Easy to spell.
4. Matches the company/product.
5. Works for future enterprise customers.

Recommended setup:

- Buy the primary domain.
- Turn on auto-renew.
- Use strong password and 2FA.
- Keep domain ownership under the company account.
- Connect domain to Cloudflare.
- Use the domain for public website.
- Keep the protected app separate from the public website.

Important separation:

- Public website: marketing, pilot interest, contact.
- Protected app: private user access only.
- GitHub/source code: private and restricted.
- Internal documents: not public.

## 9. Insurance Considerations

Before paid pilots or customer agreements, review insurance with a qualified broker.

Insurance to consider:

- General liability.
- Professional liability / errors and omissions.
- Cyber liability.
- Technology errors and omissions.
- Directors and officers insurance later if investors or board members are involved.

Why it matters:

- Prometheus may handle business activity data.
- Prometheus may handle location verification data.
- Customers may rely on reporting.
- A customer may ask about insurance before signing.
- Insurance makes the business look more enterprise-ready.

Do not overbuy too early, but know what coverage will be required before serious paid contracts.

- Tighten support process.
- Confirm legal and insurance needs.
- Improve public website.
- Build domain-based credibility.
- Continue founder pressure testing.
- Decide whether to pursue more pilots, a paid founding partner, or investor conversations.

## Highest-Priority Rule

Prometheus should not look bigger than it is, but it should operate cleaner than expected for an early-stage startup.

The goal is simple:

- Protect the founder.
- Protect the company.
- Protect customer trust.
- Keep records clean.
- Make pilot approval easier.
- Be ready to accept money the right way when the opportunity appears.
