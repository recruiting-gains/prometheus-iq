## Important Note

Most field-sales software companies are private, so exact revenue is usually not public. Pricing from company websites is stronger evidence than revenue estimates. Revenue numbers from databases like Growjo should be treated as directional estimates, not audited facts.

## Takeaways For Prometheus

1. Competitors have been working on field sales execution for roughly 10+ years.
2. Pricing commonly lands around $50-$130 per user per month when public.
3. Enterprise/custom plans are common.
4. Add-ons and AI features are common upsell paths.
5. Estimated revenue for several private competitors sits in the low single-digit millions to high teens of millions annually.
6. This market is real, but it is not winner-take-all.

## Prometheus Pricing Implication

Prometheus should not try to be the cheapest forever. It should start with a focused wedge and clear ROI.

Possible early pricing after proof:

- Solo/rep version: $19-$39/user/month.
- Small team/gym pilot: $99-$299/month per club.
- Multi-rep business plan: $49-$79/user/month.
- Enterprise Fitness Connection pilot: custom monthly subscription.

If Prometheus proves a meaningful lift in units, pricing should be based on value, not just competitor discounts.

## Strategic Positioning

The competitor landscape proves customers already pay for:

- Route planning.
- Field sales activity tracking.
- Territory mapping.
- Mileage/gas savings.
- Manager visibility.
- AI recommendations.

Prometheus must win by being sharper:

- Built for local-store-marketing sales.
- Simple enough for reps to use every day.
- Focused on units and route learning.
- Designed to create leads before HubSpot/Salesforce sees them.
- Proven first with real Fitness Connection data.
